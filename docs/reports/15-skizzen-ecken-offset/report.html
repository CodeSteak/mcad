2 corner() in sketches

Rules (plan 17.9): corner() between two segments rounds the corner between them – the closing line of close() counts as a segment, so corner() before close() rounds the corner to the closing line; corner() directly after start(p) rounds the start corner (between the last and the first segment); likewise corner() after the last segment of a contour that ends at the start (no closing line needed). The sketch is first built without corners (tangential arcs and line(length) refer to the sharp corner), then all corners of a contour are computed on the original segments; if two corners need overlapping parts of one segment, both fail with the largest common value – independent of the order. The fillet is a segment of the corner line (highlighting); a start corner is placed at the end of the contour. Chamfer: intersection points at distance d from the corner point.

3 fillet2d and chamfer2d

fillet2d(r) { profil; zone; … } / chamfer2d(d): the first child is the profile (any origin: square, polygon, sketches, 2D Booleans), further children are zones (2D shapes and zone(linie | ebene, d)). Corners are joints with a kink, convex and concave; the fillet lies on the inner side of the kink (convex: material removed, concave: material added). Selection as in 3D: select = "inside" (default) – the corner point lies in a zone; "touch" – also when only the replaced piece of the profile touches a zone. Inline hint "n Ecken" ("n corners"), an empty selection is a warning, radii that are too large are errors per corner (the remaining corners are rounded). Nested blocks act from the inside out; the transitions of a fillet are tangential and do not count. A single closed contour keeps its segments (splines stay splines); everything else goes through the exact region.

// Purpose: angle bracket with a filleted profile (plan 17.3) – L profile as a polygon; `fillet2d`
// with a zone rounds only the inner corner with a large radius (notch effect), an outer `fillet2d`

// and do not count); extruded, with slots from a `stroke` of a line segment (round ends).

}

4 offset: semantics and comparison with OpenSCAD

As in OpenSCAD 2021.01: first positional argument r, without arguments r = 1, r wins over delta, chamfer only with delta. r > 0: convex corners become exact arcs, concave ones stay sharp; r < 0: convex ones stay sharp, concave ones become round, narrow parts disappear, holes grow. delta: corners extended to the intersection of the offset edges (without a miter limit – OpenSCAD sets miter_limit 106, as the acute triangle below confirms), chamfer = true cuts like Clipper's jtSquare perpendicular to the angle bisector at distance delta from the corner point (measured in OpenSCAD: 196 − 4·(2√2 − 2)² for the square). Exact: region ∪ (bands ± r along the segments ∪ circles at the corners), or region ∖ (…) for r < 0 – the Minkowski sum with the circle, made of lines and arcs; delta: one-sided bands and corner pieces (miter/chamfer) at diverging corners.

5 stroke

stroke(w, cap = "round" | "flat" | "square", join = "round" | "miter" | "bevel") (default round/round): children are open sketches (paths; a contour that ends at its start is closed) and arbitrary 2D profiles (their boundary as closed paths: stroke(1) circle(5) is a ring). Bands of half the width along the segments (line: rectangle, arc: annular sector), joins round, mitered (up to 4 half-widths like SVG's stroke-miterlimit, otherwise bevel) or beveled, ends round, flat or extended by w/2; crossing paths are united.

// the floor from it (full width, flat ends) and the walls (difference with a narrower
// stroke whose square ends reach beyond the duct: the ends stay open); plus

6 Kernel: exact 2D regions

  • region2::combine(operanden, prädikat): all segments are split at their exact intersection points (line–line, line–circle, circle–circle including tangency, overlap of collinear lines and identical circles; points within 10−9 of the size are merged), duplicate pieces are dropped. Each piece is kept where the predicate differs on its left and right (test points next to the middle of the piece; winding number per fill rule via the ray towards +x, arcs split into y-monotone pieces), with the region on the left. Joined into loops with the tightest turn at shared vertices; pieces of the same line or circle are merged. Result: outer loops counterclockwise, holes clockwise, only lines and arcs.
  • 2D subtrees: the predicate is the Boolean expression of the leaves (fill rule per leaf: polygons even-odd, otherwise nonzero); a randomized test compares 60 Booleans of circles, rectangles, polygons and sketches with the polygon clipping of the display (≤ 10−3).
  • B-splines enter as G1 biarcs (two tangential arcs per piece, pieces halved until the deviation is ≤ 1 µm, at most 10−5 of the model size). Rationale: the offset of a B-spline is not a B-spline, the offset of an arc is an arc – the offset is exact with respect to the approximation and tangent-continuous, intersections stay solvable in closed form, STEP contains lines and circles; 1 µm is far below printing and manufacturing tolerances and above the computational accuracy.
  • Engine: Op::Offset, Op::Blend2, Op::Stroke are 2D nodes; profile::lift computes the region (cached by subtree content) and passes it on as sketch leaves – extrude, revolve, loft, sweep and STEP see exact lines and arcs. cad_engine::region_2d returns the region of a 2D node (bounding boxes for the compatibility tests).

Fix from step 1 (arcs over 90°). The facade builds an arc as a rational quadratic B-spline with spans ≤ 90°; a longer arc is a curve of several spans. Booleans with extrusions of such curves failed in SMLib depending on position (ring of two 180° arcs ∪ box, rotated by 157.5°: "volume 924.12 impossible for operands 976.79, 43.2"; correct with 4 × 90°). The engine now splits arcs over 90° into equal pieces ≤ 90° before the kernel (profile::split_arcs; extrude, revolve, sweep – not loft); a half circle becomes two cylindrical faces. Regression test ring_of_long_arcs_unites_with_a_box: arcs of 180°, 120°, 90° and 360°, each with eight box positions, volume = 3 × exact 2D area of the union (≤ 10−5). Found along the way: the winding number of the region took arc end points from the angles (rounding ~10−15), so a ray exactly through a vertex counted wrongly (ring of three 120° arcs without a hole) – now the shared vertices of the loop are used (test rings_of_arcs_of_any_sweep).

10 Limitations

  • offset(delta) on arcs extends the tangents (miter of the tangent lines) – OpenSCAD only has polygons there. B-splines enter offset, stroke and 2D Booleans as biarcs (1 µm); the result contains arcs instead of the spline; corner and fillet2d of a single contour keep the spline.
  • fillet2d/chamfer2d have no radius handle (only corner does); zones are 2D shapes and zone(linie | ebene); 3D zones are ignored with a warning.
  • The region classifies pieces via test points at a distance of ~10−6 of the size: boundaries that come closer than that without intersecting or overlapping can be classified wrongly. Candidate pairs via an x sort (quadratic in the worst case).
  • 2D Booleans between results (difference() { stroke(…); stroke(…); }) remain 3D Booleans of the extruded parts as before; offset, fillet2d, stroke compute exactly in 2D (over their whole subtree).
  • stroke: miter limit fixed at 4 half-widths; an arc with a radius below half the width is swept as a sector plus a mirrored sector. Errors of a 2D operation under an extrusion appear at both lines (as before for 2D errors).
  • Lofts join sketches segment by segment and still receive arcs over 90° as curves of several spans; whether SMLib Booleans with such loft faces have the same weakness has not been checked.
  • cad render --line N of a corner line highlights the whole sketch (highlighting of the single segment only in the UI, as in report 14).
